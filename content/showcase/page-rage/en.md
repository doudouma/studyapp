## What it is

Page Rage is a cathartic browser game and browser extension by u/alexreardon — a front-end engineer known for his work on the `pragmatic-drag-and-drop` library — that lets you obliterate the elements of any web page. Playable on [page-rage.com](https://page-rage.com/) or installable as an extension that can tear apart whatever page you are currently on. It is the product of a long-term hobby project: hundreds of hours of work driven by an exploration of extreme front-end web performance optimization.

## Core value proposition

* **An arsenal for any page**: Multiple interactive weapons — a flamethrower, a gravity gun, and black hole/wormhole mechanics — that interact with real page elements in a physics sandbox.
* **Systems that react to each other**: The simulation is coherent, not scripted; flames drawn from the flamethrower are physically sucked into active black holes.
* **Juice everywhere**: Bullet-time slowdown, custom sound effects, mood-setting music, and mathematically generated real-time VFX such as dynamic portal lightning.
* **Zero-friction destruction**: The site version plays instantly in the browser, and the extension destroys any active page without requiring intrusive page permissions.

## Current realities and limitations

* **Closed source**: The project incorporates licensed assets, so the performance tricks and the custom physics engine remain a black box until the promised technical deep dives are published.
* **A toy, not a progression game**: Destruction is the whole loop — there is no evidence of goals, saves, or campaign structure, so session value lives entirely in the sandbox chaos.
* **Single-maintainer cadence**: A solo hobby project means content updates and fixes move at one person's pace.

## Verdict

> **Primary recommendation**
> Play it the moment you have a bad day with a web page. For developers, it is also worth watching for the promised deep dives: an extreme-performance sandbox built by the author of a widely used drag-and-drop library is a rare chance to learn tricks that usually stay inside game-engine land. Just don't expect an open-source codebase — enjoy the chaos instead.

## Licensing, openness, and what's next

The project is closed source because it incorporates licensed assets, which makes full open-sourcing difficult. The creator plans to release technical deep dives — via blog posts or videos — covering the performance tricks and the physics engine under the hood.

## Community reception and key debates

The release hit a nostalgia nerve across the developer community, drawing direct lines to the destruction-toy lineage:

* **Classic inspirations**: Comparisons came immediately to [Desktop Destroyer](https://gamia-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Desktop_Destroyer_Game), the iconic 1990s Windows desktop smash-up toy, and to Alan Becker's *Animator vs. Animation* series.
* **The web destruction canon**: Veterans recalled early web hubs like Newgrounds and Stick Death, plus DOM-destruction predecessors [kickassapp.com](https://kickassapp.com/) and [playanypage.com](https://playanypage.com/).
* **Craft over AI**: The community read the "AI as assistant, art by hand" split as a healthy model — AI for investigations and acceleration, humans for the pixels players actually see.
