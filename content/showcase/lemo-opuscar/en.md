## It turned Opus 5.5-made videos into a style library

Every platform is currently boosting code videos, and accounts have hit ten thousand followers in a month and millions of views on them. The method is no mystery: one Claude Opus 5.5 and a topic, and you can start. The HalfAI article we reference shares the Skill that makes it easy — Lemo-Opuscar.

Its strongest point is not "it makes videos", but that it has organized the video content people have made with Opus 5.5 into ready-made, named styles. You no longer design a style from scratch — install the Skill, say which style you want, hand it your topic and material, and it generates video in that style.

Every demo film in the repo has visuals and a score written by Claude as code, with the voiceover synthesized by TTS: no video-generation models, no stock footage. Each style is a reusable prompt plus a short film directed, drawn and scored entirely in code. The GIFs below were captured by HalfAI after actually running the repo.

## The style gallery: 11 categories, one by one

### Editing & documentary · 8 styles

Feed it real footage and it cuts the material into news visuals. Captioned news is what many news accounts use — understandable even on mute; the real-time countdown split screen suits rocket-launch coverage, tiles counting from 1 to 9 down to ignition; the sports tactics board freezes the frame, draws lines and splits screens, with every player's position and trajectory clear; TV news packaging is a whole station's opening titles, usable for daily or morning briefings. This category also has 1920s newsreels, timelapse symphony, visual investigation and constructivist montage — visual investigation walks through a single object or event in detail, great for explainer content.

## Why it's worth keeping

The article author's own words: "Honestly, this is the first time I've seen a repo like this." The value isn't the prompts themselves — it solves a specific problem: some people know exactly what feeling they want but don't have the style's name or concept in their head. With a style library indexed by name, you stop asking the AI to invent a style from zero.

## Turn it into your own style

A recently added feature in the repo: feed in your brand colors, logo, IP and account tone, and turn a built-in style into yours. For example, the repo author turned Swiss kinetic typography into "Material grid" — keeping the grid, the beat and the fonts, but swapping in real objects made of clay, plastic, velvet, glass and chrome.

![The remade style: Material grid (built on Swiss kinetic typography)](/showcase/lemo-material-grid.gif)

The remaking process is one question at a time: each question gives a few options, recommended first; it shows you a style image, then cuts a 15-to-30-second demo film, and only saves it as your named style once you're satisfied — after that, you just call it by name.

## Install and use

Completely free and open source. It installs into Claude Code with two terminal commands (HalfAI tested the install):

```
claude plugin marketplace add lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar
claude plugin install lemo-opuscar@lemolab
```

![The tested install: marketplace add + plugin install, two steps](/showcase/lemo-install.jpg)

Then just say what film you want — style names work in Chinese. The README's example:

```
Make a 30-second film in oil impasto style about my orange cat that waits on the windowsill every day for me to come home from work.
```

If you haven't picked a film yet, run `/lemo-opuscar:lemo-opuscar` and it asks first whether you want to make a film with a library style, remake a style as your own, or use a style you've saved.

## Before you start

Three preconditions, stated plainly in the article:

* **Styles are tied to Opus 5.5.** Every style was tuned against Claude Opus 5.5; other models give no guarantee.
* **Time and tokens are real.** One film takes the agent roughly 30 to 60 minutes, and the token usage is not small.
* **Environment requirements.** You need Node 20+, ffmpeg and Python 3.11+ on the machine.

## Verdict

> **Use it as a style index, not a video generator.**
> The real product is "a style's name + a reusable prompt + one all-code demo film" — the video is just how the demo is presented. The right way to pick a style is to watch the demos first; the felt difference between categories is much larger than the names suggest. Before you start, keep three things in mind: Opus 5.5 only, 30–60 minutes per film, and non-trivial token cost.
